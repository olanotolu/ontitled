## Glossary

### A

**API (Application Programming Interface)**
The interface that allows software systems to communicate with Themis. The Themis REST API enables programmatic access to quotes, contracts, outcomes, and verification.

### B

**Base Compute Cost**
The raw cost of GPU compute required to execute an AI task, sourced from the Ornn Compute Price Index. The foundation of Themis pricing calculations.

**Behavioral Equivalence**
A quality standard where an AI system's outputs match the distribution of behaviors of a reference model. Used by some optimizers (like Ship) to ensure consistent results.

### C

**Capability Equivalence**
A guarantee that any problem solved by a reference model will also be solved by the optimized system. A Ship optimization concept.

**Contract**
A UPX contract is a binding agreement for a specific number of verified outcomes at a fixed price. Contracts define the ticker, quantity, period, and terms.

**Cost Per Outcome**
The total cost divided by verified outcomes. The key metric for comparing AI vendor efficiency. Different from cost per token.

**Cryptographic Proof**
An immutable, mathematically verifiable record of a verification. Generated by Dike using SHA-256 hashes and Ed25519 signatures.

### D

**Dike**
The independent verification protocol. Named for the Greek goddess of justice, Dike checks enterprise systems of record to verify AI outcomes actually occurred.

**Disintermediation**
The risk that buyers and vendors bypass Themis after an initial successful transaction. Mitigated by network effects and trust advantages.

**Domain (UPX)**
The business domain of a UPX contract (e.g., CX for Customer Experience, SWE for Software Engineering).

### E

**Enterprise System of Record**
The authoritative system where business data lives (Zendesk, Salesforce, Jira, etc.). Dike reads from these systems to verify outcomes.

### F

**Failure (Verification)**
When an outcome does not pass Dike verification. Common reasons: customer reopen, human intervention, ticket not actually resolved.

### G

**Goodhart's Law**
"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." The risk that AI agents optimize for passing verification rather than solving problems. Dike mitigates this through multi-factor verification.

**Ground Truth**
Objective, verifiable facts about whether work was completed. Dike establishes ground truth by checking systems of record.

### H

**Hallucination**
When an AI generates false or nonsensical information. In the context of Themis, a hallucinated resolution is when an agent claims a ticket is resolved when it is not.

### I

**Inference-Time Optimization**
Techniques to improve AI performance at runtime (e.g., routing to cheaper models, speculative decoding). Ship and similar tools specialize in this.

**Input Layer**
The layer of AI infrastructure concerned with executing requests efficiently (compute, tokens, routing). Contrast with output layer.

### J

**Jevons Paradox**
The economic phenomenon where increased efficiency leads to increased consumption. As AI gets cheaper, more tasks become economical.

### L

**Loop (Infinite)**
When an AI agent repeatedly attempts a task without converging on a solution. Traditional billing charges for all attempts; Themis charges only for verified outcomes.

### M

**Model-Agnostic**
Not tied to any specific AI model. Themis is model-agnostic — it works with Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, or any other model.

### O

**Oracle (Themis Phase 1)**
The initial phase of Themis: retrospective auditing of AI vendor performance. Provides proof of waste to justify UPX contracts.

**Ornn Compute Price Index (OCPI)**
Real-time pricing data for GPU compute (H100, A100, etc.). Themis uses Ornn to calculate base compute costs.

**Outcome**
A unit of verified AI work. Examples: one resolved support ticket, one merged PR, one qualified lead.

**Outcome-Based Pricing**
Paying for verified results rather than inputs (tokens, compute). The core Themis value proposition.

**Output Layer**
The layer of AI infrastructure concerned with verifying and settling outcomes. Themis operates at the output layer.

### P

**Proof (Cryptographic)**
See Cryptographic Proof.

### Q

**Quality SLA**
The minimum verification rate required for a contract (e.g., 95%). If vendors fall below this, they may face penalties or contract termination.

**Quote**
A price estimate for a UPX contract. Quotes are valid for 24 hours and include detailed pricing breakdowns.

### R

**Region (UPX)**
The geographic or compliance zone for a contract (US, EU, GLB, etc.). Affects data residency and pricing.

**Reopen**
When a customer reopens a previously resolved ticket. A common cause of verification failure.

**Retry Multiplier**
The factor accounting for failed AI attempts before success. Calculated as 1 / (verification_rate × efficiency).

**Risk Premium**
A buffer in pricing to protect vendors from compute price volatility during the contract period.

**Routing**
The process of selecting which AI vendor executes a specific task. Themis optimizes routing for cost and quality.

### S

**Settlement**
The payment of funds to a vendor after verification. Settlement is conditional on passing Dike verification.

**Ship**
An inference optimization tool from Thesean that reduces LLM costs by 50%. Optimizes the input layer.

**SLA (Service Level Agreement)**
The window of time (typically 72 hours) during which Dike verifies an outcome. Also refers to quality commitments.

**State Delta**
A change in system state (e.g., ticket moving from "open" to "resolved"). Dike verifies state deltas as evidence of work completion.

**System of Record**
See Enterprise System of Record.

### T

**Task (UPX)**
The specific type of work in a UPX contract (RES for resolution, ISS for issue, etc.).

**Telemetry**
Technical data about AI execution (model calls, latency, errors). Optional data vendors can submit to Themis.

**Temporal Verification**
Verifying that an outcome stays valid over time (the 72-hour window). Prevents premature ticket closures.

**Themis**
The routing and settlement layer for AI outcomes. Named for the Greek Titaness of divine law and order.

**Ticker (UPX)**
The standardized identifier for a UPX contract (e.g., UPX-CX-RES-US-L2).

**Token**
A unit of text processing in LLMs (roughly 0.75 words). Traditional billing charges per token; Themis charges per outcome.

### U

**UPX (Universal Product Exchange)**
The standardized contract format for machine labor outcomes. Enables pricing, routing, and settlement of AI work.

### V

**Vendor**
An AI provider (Sierra, Intercom, custom agent) that executes tasks and submits outcomes for verification.

**Verified (Status)**
An outcome that has passed Dike verification. Payment is cleared for verified outcomes.

**Verification**
The process of independently checking whether claimed work was actually completed. Core to the Themis value proposition.

**Verification Rate**
The percentage of claimed outcomes that pass verification. Key quality metric.

**Verification Window**
See SLA.

### W

**White Space**
The unoccupied market position Themis targets: independent, buyer-controlled, cross-vendor verification + settlement.

## FAQ — For CFOs

### General Questions

**Q: What exactly is Themis?**

A: Themis is a platform that lets you buy AI work by the outcome instead of by the token. Instead of paying for compute hours or API calls, you pay a fixed price for each verified result — like a resolved support ticket or a merged code PR. We verify the work was actually done before you pay.

**Q: How is this different from paying OpenAI or Anthropic directly?**

A: When you pay OpenAI directly, you pay for every token the AI consumes — including failed attempts, infinite loops, and hallucinations. With Themis, you pay only for verified outcomes. If the AI fails, the vendor absorbs the cost, not you.

**Q: What happens if an AI vendor claims they resolved a ticket but they didn't?**

A: Dike, our verification system, checks your actual Zendesk/Salesforce to confirm the ticket was resolved. If the customer reopens it or a human has to fix it, verification fails and you don't pay.

### Pricing Questions

**Q: How is the price per outcome calculated?**

A: The price includes:
1. Base compute cost (from Ornn real-time pricing)
2. Retry multiplier (accounts for failed attempts)
3. Verification cost (Dike checking your systems)
4. Risk premium (buffer for price volatility)
5. Themis fee (platform fee)

**Q: Can I predict my monthly spend?**

A: Yes — that's a core benefit. With UPX contracts, you pre-purchase a fixed number of outcomes at a fixed price. Your spend is capped at the contract value. No surprises.

**Q: What if compute prices spike during my contract?**

A: The risk premium in your quote protects vendors from moderate price swings. For extreme spikes (>50%), contracts may have renegotiation clauses. Your price is locked for the contract period.

**Q: Is Themis more expensive than paying for tokens directly?**

A: It depends on your current waste. If your AI vendors rarely fail, direct token pricing might be cheaper. But if you have retries, hallucinations, or reopens, Themis often works out cheaper because you don't pay for failures. We offer free audits to help you compare.

### Contract Questions

**Q: What is a UPX contract?**

A: A UPX contract is a standardized agreement to purchase a specific type of AI outcome (like US support resolutions) at a fixed price. It's like a futures contract for AI work.

**Q: What if I don't use all my contracted outcomes?**

A: Unused outcomes expire at the end of the contract period. We recommend starting with conservative quantities and scaling up as you understand your usage patterns.

**Q: Can I cancel a contract?**

A: Contracts are binding for the specified period. However, if a vendor consistently fails to meet quality SLAs, you may have grounds for early termination.

**Q: What happens when my contract runs low?**

A: The dashboard shows a "low balance" warning when you're below 20% remaining. You can request a new batch anytime. We recommend requesting 30 days before depletion.

### Verification Questions

**Q: How does Dike verify outcomes?**

A: Dike connects to your systems (Zendesk, Salesforce, etc.) and checks:
1. Was the ticket marked resolved?
2. Did it stay resolved for 72 hours?
3. Did any human have to fix it?
4. Did the customer reopen it?

Only if all checks pass is the outcome verified.

**Q: Why 72 hours?**

A: Our data shows 96% of premature closures happen within 72 hours. This window catches most problems while keeping verification timely.

**Q: Can I customize the verification criteria?**

A: Standard UPX contracts use the 72-hour window. Enterprise customers can negotiate custom SLAs for specific use cases.

**Q: What if I disagree with a verification result?**

A: Vendors (not buyers) can dispute verification results. If you believe there's a system error, contact support and we'll investigate.

## FAQ — For Engineers

### Integration Questions

**Q: What APIs do I need to integrate?**

A: For enterprise buyers: typically just webhook endpoints to receive verification results. For vendors: the outcomes API to submit work for verification.

**Q: How do I connect Zendesk to Themis?**

A: See the [Zendesk Integration Guide](./06-integration-zendesk.md). It involves OAuth setup, webhook configuration, and optional app installation.

**Q: What data does Themis access in our systems?**

A: Read-only access to ticket status, comments, and metadata. We never write to your systems or access sensitive customer data.

**Q: Is there an SDK?**

A: Yes — official SDKs for Python, JavaScript, and Go. See [API Reference](./05-api-reference.md) for details.

### Technical Questions

**Q: How does Themis routing work?**

A: We use convex optimization to select the best vendor for each task based on cost, quality, and latency constraints. See [Pricing Mathematics](./03-pricing-mathematics.md) for details.

**Q: What if Themis goes down?**

A: Themis is designed for high availability with multi-region redundancy. If outages occur, vendor payments may be delayed but no outcomes are lost. Verification resumes when service is restored.

**Q: How are cryptographic proofs generated?**

A: Using SHA-256 for state hashing and Ed25519 for digital signatures. Each proof is immutable and independently verifiable.

**Q: Can we self-host Dike?**

A: Enterprise customers in regulated industries can deploy Dike as an on-premise sidecar. Contact sales for details.

## FAQ — For Vendors

### Getting Started

**Q: How do I become a Themis vendor?**

A: Apply at ontitled.ai/vendors/apply. We review your capabilities, security practices, and integration compatibility. Approval typically takes 1-2 weeks.

**Q: What are the requirements?**

A: HTTPS API endpoint, ability to submit outcomes with telemetry, and webhook handler for verification results. See [Vendor Integration Guide](./07-integration-vendors.md).

**Q: Is there a cost to be a vendor?**

A: No upfront cost. Themis takes a percentage of each verified outcome as a platform fee.

### Payment Questions

**Q: When do I get paid?**

A: After Dike verification (72 hours after claim, assuming success). Payments are batched and transferred via ACH or wire.

**Q: What if verification fails?**

A: You don't get paid for failed outcomes. This incentivizes quality work. Analyze failure reasons in your vendor dashboard to improve.

**Q: Can I dispute a failed verification?**

A: Yes, within 7 days. Submit evidence through the vendor portal. Our team reviews and makes a determination.

### Performance Questions

**Q: How can I improve my verification rate?**

A: Focus on first-contact resolution. Common failure reasons are customer reopens and human intervention. Better initial resolution = higher verification rate.

**Q: How does Themis routing allocate traffic?**

A: Based on your historical verification rate, cost, and latency. Higher quality vendors get more traffic. See [Pricing Mathematics](./03-pricing-mathematics.md).

**Q: Can I see my performance metrics?**

A: Yes — vendor dashboard shows verification rate, cost per outcome, routing allocation, and failure reasons.

## Troubleshooting Guide

### Issue: Outcome not appearing in dashboard

**Check:**
1. Was the outcome submitted successfully? (Check API response)
2. Is the contract still active?
3. Was the ticker correct?

**Resolution:**
```bash
# Check outcome status
curl https://api.ontitled.ai/v1/outcomes/{outcome_id} \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer {api_key}"
```

### Issue: Verification taking longer than 72 hours

**Check:**
1. Is the SLA window actually expired? (72 hours from claim time)
2. Is there a system backlog? (Check status page)

**Resolution:** Contact support if > 75 hours elapsed.

### Issue: Quote seems too high

**Check:**
1. Verify ticker and quantity
2. Check if risk premium is inflated (long contract period)
3. Compare to historical quotes

**Resolution:** Request a new quote or negotiate terms.

### Issue: API returning 429 (Rate Limited)

**Check:**
1. Are you exceeding your plan's rate limits?
2. Are you retrying failed requests too aggressively?

**Resolution:** Implement exponential backoff. Consider upgrading plan.

### Issue: Webhook not receiving events

**Check:**
1. Is your endpoint publicly accessible?
2. Is SSL certificate valid?
3. Are you returning 200 status?

**Resolution:** Check webhook logs in dashboard. Test with curl.
